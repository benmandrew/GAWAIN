# RERS 2016/2017: the mistranslated pattern properties

The RERS 2016 and 2017 sequential LTL tracks contain 105 properties whose formula does not say what their English gloss says. Each one has the shape `false R (Q & ψ)`, that is G(Q ∧ ψ), where the gloss describes a scoped Dwyer pattern G(Q → ψ). The conjunction needs Q at every position, so every one of these properties is false on its model. This note records how the 105 cases were checked and how they became the benchmark set `bench/sets/rers-precedes.csv`, in which the intended pattern is the weakening of the shipped formula.

## Counts and patterns

The script `bench/rers_precedes.py` finds the cases with the regular expression `^\(false R \(([io][A-Z]) & (.*)\)\)$`. It finds 40 cases in 2016 (6, 6, 7, 5, 5, 3, 1, 5 and 2 over problems 1 to 9) and 65 in 2017 (7, 5, 8, 6, 9, 9, 5, 9 and 7). This confirms the counts of 40 and 65. The parallel track has no affected property: its 700 properties carry no gloss and none has the `G(atom & …)` shape.

The name "precedes" covers only half of the set. The glosses match three patterns from Dwyer, Avrunin and Corbett, "Patterns in Property Specifications for Finite-State Verification" (ICSE 1999, catalogue at <https://matthewbdwyer.github.io/psp/>). There are 54 cases of *precedence after Q until R*, `G((Q & !R) -> (!S W (P | R)))`. There are 35 cases of *response after Q until R*, `G((Q & !R) -> ((P -> (!R U (S & !R))) W R))`. The remaining 16 are the *two-cause response chain*, `G((P & X F T) -> X F(T & F S))`. The fix replaces `(Q &` with `(! Q |`. Spot 2.15.1 shows for all 105 cases that the fix is equivalent to the pattern built from the gloss, that φ implies the fix and that the fix does not imply φ. The last result holds in plain LTL and also under the RERS trace assumption that exactly one atom holds at each position.

## Semantics

RERS reads a property over the infinite runs of the program, and an invalid input ends the run. On 2016 problem 1 this reading reproduces all 100 official verdicts. The other reading, in which the program ignores an invalid input and continues, gives 14 mismatches. Both routes below use the first reading.

## Routes to a verdict

No verdict in the set is a guess. Each comes from one of three sound routes, and the build stops if two routes disagree.

**Explicit search.** `bench/rers_seq.py` compiles the C program with an input harness and explores its state space breadth-first, with the `.data` section as the state. It then builds a *known-runs automaton*: the explored graph plus every official counterexample lasso that replays as a real run. A formula fails when Spot finds a run of this automaton that violates it. It holds only when the exploration was complete. Only 2016/1 (51 states), 2017/1 (493) and 2017/2 (315) are complete. On those three problems the route matches all 100 official verdicts on each. The other 15 problems stop at the 300,000-state cap. Of the official lassos, between 44 of 74 (2017/5) and all of them replay as real runs, 1,030 of 1,179 in total. A lasso that does not replay within 3000 loop rounds is left out, and it costs only completeness. On av3, with about 10 other processes running, a problem took between 35 s and 936 s.

**Implication from the official verdicts.** If an officially true property ψ satisfies ψ ∧ one-event → fix, the fix holds. If ψ ∧ one-event → ¬fix, no run satisfies the fix.

**nuXmv.** `bench/rers_seq_smv.py` translates the C program into an SMV model and checks it with nuXmv 2.1.0 IC3 (`check_ltlspec_ic3`). The programs are tables of event-condition-action rules, and the translator refuses any file outside that shape. Globals that take part in arithmetic become `signed word[32]`, whose division, `mod` and overflow match C compiled with `-fwrapv` (checked with `-7 / 2`, `-7 mod 2`, `7 / -2`, `7 mod -2` and `2000000000 * 3`). Constant arrays and pointers to them become case expressions. A separate invariant `G !oob` must hold, which shows that no executed array read is out of bounds. A problem's nuXmv verdicts are used only when that invariant holds and every decided shipped property matches its official verdict. On 2016/1 and 2017/1, nuXmv matches all 100 official verdicts on each and agrees with the explicit route on every fix. On 2017/2 it decides only 7 checks within the time limit, and all 7 agree. On 2017/4, IC3 decided 14 of 25 checks within 3 hours per session, all in agreement.

## Results

All 105 shipped formulas are *degenerate*: M ⊨ ¬φ. Spot proves this for every model at once, because φ ∧ one-event ∧ GF(input) ∧ GF(output) is unsatisfiable, and every run of a RERS program alternates single inputs and outputs forever. The model checkers also confirm it directly on 21 cases: the explicit route on 18 and nuXmv on 17.

The intended weakenings split into 78 that fail, 15 that hold and 12 that are undecided. Every failing case has a real counterexample run from the explicit route, and nuXmv confirms 8 of them. Ten of the weakenings that hold come from the three complete problems, and nuXmv also proves 8 of these 10. The other 4 (2016/6 property 85, 2016/8 property 58, 2017/6 property 62 and 2017/7 property 39) follow by implication from an officially true property. nuXmv alone proves the fifteenth, 2017/4 property 60, in 6318 s. That proof rests on less evidence than the others: on 2017/4 the model was checked against only 6 official verdicts (5 false and property 0, true, proved in 2144 s). Seven more true properties timed out at 3 hours each. By pattern, precedence gives 37 fail, 9 hold and 8 unknown, response gives 27, 6 and 2, and the response chain gives 14, 0 and 2. No two routes disagreed on any value. In 2 of the failing cases (2016/1 property 86 and 2017/1 property 5), no run of M satisfies the fix, so the weakening is itself degenerate. The CSV records the route of each verdict in the `*_route` columns.

The 12 undecided cases are 2016/3 properties 43, 56 and 88, 2017/3 property 22, 2017/5 properties 26, 47, 61, 80, 83 and 87, 2017/6 property 92 and 2017/9 property 88. Explicit search to 3,000,000 states on 2016/3, 2017/3, 2017/4 and 2017/5 found no counterexample (about 400 s to 530 s each on av3). The same search ran out of a 12 GB cap on 2017/6 and 2017/9. nuXmv decided none of them. The runs that would have covered 2017/3 and 2017/5 were stopped after IC3 had spent 2 hours on 2016/2 and 2016/4 without finishing. Bit-level IC3 is slow on the 32-bit arithmetic: on 2017/2, whose explicit state space has only 315 states, one check took 175 s to 1759 s, and 109 of 116 checks timed out at 2400 s per session. On 2016/3 the bounds invariant alone timed out at 2000 s with LTL IC3 and at 900 s with invariant IC3. On problems 6 to 9, the model build ran out of an 8 GB cap (the SMV files are 13 MB to 118 MB). An undecided weakening has no known counterexample, but the set marks it unknown and does not count it as true.

## Reproducing

The script `bench/rers_seq.py fetch` downloads the four archives (URLs in the script) into `bench/data/rers/`. Then `sh bench/rers_seq_run.sh check 4 12 3600 OUT --only-affected --max-states 300000` runs the explicit route, `bench/rers_seq_smv.py check --out-dir OUT2` runs nuXmv, and `bench/rers_precedes.py OUT OUT2` writes the CSV. The per-problem tables used for the committed CSV are in `bench/results/rers-precedes/`.

The set is small, but each row has an intended weakening that a person wrote, and most rows have a decided verdict. That makes it a direct check that a search for weakenings can recover what the property author meant.
